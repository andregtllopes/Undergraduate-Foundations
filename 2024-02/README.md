# Semester 2024/02: Technical Density and Analytical Transition

This semester marked a significant increase in the mathematical complexity and physical modeling depth of the Aerospace Engineering curriculum. It represents the transition to the professional cycle, where analytical foundations were applied to complex systems and discrete computing.

## 📌 Overview

The core focus of this period was the mastery of continuous media, fluid dynamics, and the introduction to numerical methods. This semester also served as a strategic pivot point: the rigorous mathematical modeling required in engineering courses sparked a formal interest in Artificial Intelligence and computing, leading to my entry into the SPEED Lab to begin exploring high-impact data research.

## 🛠️ Key Academic Focus

- **Numerical Analysis (DCC033):** A paradigm shift from analytical calculus to discrete computing. Mastery of iterative methods such as Newton-Raphson and Gauss-Seidel, with a focus on error analysis and function approximation — the first steps toward understanding Machine Learning optimization.
- **Mechanics of Solids (EES022):** Comprehensive study of stress and strain states. Applied Mohr’s Circle for transformations and failure criteria like Tresca and Von Mises to predict structural integrity in aerospace components.
- **Fluid Mechanics (EMA091):** Application of conservation laws via the Reynolds Transport Theorem and the derivation of Navier-Stokes equations.
- **Modeling of Dynamic Systems (EMA089):** Frequency domain analysis using Laplace Transforms, block diagrams, and the Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion for control systems.
- **Mathematical Physics (MAT016):** Solving Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) through Fourier Series and the Separation of Variables method, essential for heat and wave modeling.
- **Circuit Analysis (ELE064):** Application of Kirchhoff's laws, Thévenin/Norton theorems, and Operational Amplifiers in signal processing.

## 🧠 Perspective

While the workload remained heavily academic, the shift toward discrete methods and system modeling provided the necessary bridge to integrate computing into the engineering workflow. The challenges faced in modeling physical phenomena this semester consolidated the "First-Principles" approach, ensuring that every simulation or algorithm is backed by a deep understanding of the underlying physics.
